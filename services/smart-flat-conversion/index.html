House-to-flats conversions

Splitting a house into self-contained flats — planning strategy, layout optimisation, structural coordination, and refinance-grade documentation.

What a flat conversion involves+

A flat conversion splits a single dwelling into two or more self-contained residential units, each with its own front door, kitchen, bathroom and living space. It's one of the most reliable value-creation strategies for property investors — a house worth £500,000 as a single dwelling can be worth £650,000-£750,000 as two self-contained flats, with ongoing rental yields significantly higher than a single let.

But the gap between a successful conversion and a failed one is the planning, the fire strategy, and the sound insulation — and all three need to be resolved before a brick is laid.

Planning+

Converting a house to flats requires full planning permission (change of use from C3 to C3 — but now subdivided). The council will assess the quality of the proposed units, the impact on neighbours, parking provision, refuse storage, and whether the property is suitable for subdivision. In conservation areas or Article 4 zones, additional scrutiny applies.

The most common reason for refusal: units that are too small or lack adequate natural light. We design to exceed the Nationally Described Space Standard from the start — because units that just meet minimums get refused, and units that exceed them get approved faster.

The refinance question+

Most investors buy, convert, and refinance — the "BRRR" strategy. The refinance valuation depends on the quality of your documentation. Bank valuers want RIBA-standard architectural drawings, Building Regulations completion certificates, and EPC ratings. Landlords who skip proper documentation lose 10-20% on refinance valuations — or can't refinance at all.

We produce refinance-grade documentation as standard. It's not an extra — it's built into the service.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need planning permission to convert a house to flats? +
Yes — full planning permission is required. The council assesses unit sizes, natural light, parking, refuse, and neighbourhood impact. We handle the full application.
How much does a flat conversion cost? +
Our design fees range from £5,000 to £15,000. Build costs are typically £3,000-£6,000 per flat for a straightforward conversion — more for high-spec finishes or significant structural work.
Will a flat conversion increase the property value? +
Typically yes. A house worth £500,000 as a single dwelling can be worth £650,000-£750,000 as two self-contained flats. The uplift depends on location, unit quality, and local demand. We can estimate the uplift during feasibility.
