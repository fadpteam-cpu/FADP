HMO conversions in London

Planning strategy, fire safety, licensing coordination and layout maximisation — for portfolio landlords and investors who want top-of-market yields.

What an HMO conversion involves+

An HMO (House in Multiple Occupation) conversion turns a family house into a shared house with individual lettable bedrooms and communal facilities. In London, where room rents range from £700 to £1,500 per month, a well-converted six-bedroom HMO can generate £5,000-£9,000 per month — significantly more than the same property let as a single dwelling.

But the gap between a compliant, well-designed HMO and an illegal, poorly-designed one is the difference between a sustainable investment and an enforcement notice. The rules are specific, the penalties are real, and London boroughs are actively enforcing.

The regulatory framework+

Planning. Converting a family house (Use Class C3) to an HMO with three to six occupants (Use Class C4) requires planning permission in most London boroughs, because most have Article 4 directions removing the permitted development right for this change of use. Converting to a large HMO (seven or more occupants) requires planning permission everywhere — it's Sui Generis use.

Licensing. All HMOs in London require either a mandatory HMO licence (for properties with five or more occupants forming two or more households) or an additional licence (varies by borough — many London boroughs require licensing for all HMOs regardless of size). We coordinate the licensing application alongside the planning and design.

Fire safety. HMO fire safety requirements are significantly more onerous than for a family house. You'll need fire doors to every habitable room, a protected escape route, emergency lighting, fire detection and alarm systems (typically LD1 grade), and in some cases automatic suppression (sprinklers). The fire strategy affects the design from day one — it's not an add-on.

Layout matters more than you think+

The difference between an HMO that lets at the top of the market and one that lets at the bottom is usually the layout, not the finishes. En-suite ratios are the biggest single factor — a six-bed HMO with six en-suites commands £100-£200 more per room per month than one with shared bathrooms. That's £7,000-£14,000 per year of additional income from the same building.

We design HMO layouts against the local rental market, not against minimum HMO standards. Meeting minimums is the definition of a lettable-but-forgettable unit.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need planning permission for an HMO conversion? +
Almost always in London, yes. Most boroughs have Article 4 directions requiring planning permission for C3 to C4 conversion (3-6 occupants). Large HMOs (7+ occupants) always need permission. We handle the application.
How much does an HMO conversion cost? +
Our design fees range from £4,000 to £12,000. Build costs are typically £4,000-£8,000 per bedroom for a compliant conversion. A six-bed HMO conversion typically costs £30,000-£60,000 total build cost.
What is the difference between a small HMO and a large HMO? +
A small HMO has 3-6 occupants forming 2+ households (Use Class C4). A large HMO has 7+ occupants (Sui Generis — requires full planning permission, additional licensing, and more stringent fire safety). Don't creep across the 7-person line without full permissions.
What yield can I expect from an HMO? +
A well-designed London HMO with high en-suite ratios typically generates 8-12% gross yield — significantly higher than a single let (3-5%). The difference between average and top-of-market yields is usually the layout design, not the location or the finishes.
