Wrap-around extensions in London

The combined rear-and-side extension — one continuous L-shaped volume that completely reworks the ground floor.

What a wrap-around extension is+

A wrap-around extension combines a rear extension and a side return infill into a single L-shaped volume. Instead of extending in one direction, you extend in two — out to the rear and across into the side return — creating one continuous open-plan ground floor that wraps around the corner of the house.

On a typical London Victorian or Edwardian terrace, this is the most complete transformation of the ground floor available without digging a basement. The kitchen, dining and living spaces merge into a single generous room. The garden-facing elevation opens up with full-height glazing across the width. Light reaches the centre of the house from two directions.

It's more expensive than either a side return or a rear extension alone, but the per-metre cost is often lower than doing both separately because you share foundations, roofing and structural elements.

Do you need planning permission?+

Almost always, yes. A wrap-around typically exceeds the permitted development limits because of its combined footprint. You'll need a householder planning application (£548 council fee, 8-13 weeks determination). In conservation areas, the application requires a more detailed Design and Access Statement and typically takes longer.

We handle the entire planning process — drawings, application forms, Design and Access Statement, officer negotiations, condition discharge.

What it costs+

Our design fees for a wrap-around extension range from £5,000 for a straightforward scheme to £20,000 for a complex project with basement coordination or listed building consent. Build costs in London are typically £2,400-£3,800 per square metre — a typical wrap-around on a Victorian terrace (25-40sqm) costs £60,000-£150,000 to build.

The design challenge+

The hardest part of a wrap-around isn't the structure or the planning — it's the corner. Where the rear extension meets the side return, you have an internal corner that can feel cramped if handled badly or feel like the hinge of the whole room if handled well. The glazing treatment at this corner — whether it's a frameless glass join, a structural post, or a solid brick pier — determines the character of the entire space.

We typically show three different approaches to this corner in the concept design stage so you can see what each option does to the light, the view, and the feel of the room.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a wrap-around extension cost? +
Our design fees range from £5,000 to £20,000. Build costs in London are typically £2,400-£3,800/sqm — a typical 25-40sqm wrap-around costs £60,000-£150,000 to build. Often better value per metre than doing side and rear separately.
Do wrap-around extensions need planning permission? +
Almost always yes. The combined footprint usually exceeds permitted development limits. You'll need a householder planning application — £548 council fee, 8-13 weeks. We handle the entire process.
Is a wrap-around better than just a rear extension? +
It depends on what you need. A rear extension adds depth. A wrap-around adds depth AND width, creating a much more generous single space. The cost difference is typically 30-50% more than a rear alone. We test both options in feasibility so you can compare directly.
How long does a wrap-around extension take? +
4-6 months for design and planning, then 4-6 months on site. Longer than a simple rear extension because the build is more complex — two foundation runs, corner structural details, wider temporary supports during construction.
