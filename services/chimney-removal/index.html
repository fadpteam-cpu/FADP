Chimney removal

Structural design for chimney breast and stack removal — party wall coordination, Building Regulations, and getting it right first time.

Why chimney removal is more complex than it looks+

A chimney breast looks like a decorative feature. It's actually a structural wall — a mass of brickwork that extends from the foundations through every floor to the roofline, carrying its own weight plus the weight of the chimney stack above. Remove it on one floor without properly supporting the weight of the floors above, and you have a structural failure.

This is why chimney breast removal needs a structural engineer's design, a Building Regulations application, and — if the chimney is shared with a neighbour (as it is on most terraced and semi-detached houses) — party wall notices under the 1996 Act.

What the process involves+

Structural design. The engineer calculates the loads being carried by the chimney breast and designs a support system — typically a gallows bracket or a steel beam — to transfer those loads to the surrounding structure. This is specific to your house: the span, the loads, the condition of the surrounding walls all matter.

Party wall notices. If the chimney is on a party wall (shared with a neighbour), you must serve a party wall notice at least two months before starting work. The neighbour can consent, dissent (triggering the appointment of surveyors), or do nothing (which counts as dissent after 14 days). We draft and coordinate the notices.

Building Regulations. A Building Control application is required for any chimney breast removal. The inspector visits during construction to check that the structural support has been installed correctly before the brickwork below is removed.

What it costs+

Our fees for chimney breast removal coordination (structural design, party wall drafting, Building Regulations) range from £1,500 to £4,000 depending on the number of floors affected and the complexity of the party wall situation. Structural engineer's fees are additional — typically £400-£800 for chimney breast calculations.

Frequently asked questions

Can I remove a chimney breast myself? +
Legally, you can do the physical work yourself. But you still need a structural engineer's design and Building Regulations approval — and if it's on a party wall, you must serve notices on your neighbour. Doing it without these steps is both illegal and dangerous.
How much does chimney breast removal cost? +
Our coordination fee is £1,500-£4,000. Build costs are typically £1,500-£4,000 per floor for the demolition and structural support. Party wall surveyor fees (if the neighbour dissents) add £1,500-£3,000.
Do I need a party wall agreement to remove a chimney? +
If the chimney is on a shared (party) wall — which it usually is on terraces and semis — yes, you must serve a party wall notice under the 1996 Act at least two months before starting work.
