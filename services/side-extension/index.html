Side extensions in London

Reclaiming the narrow strip beside a Victorian or Edwardian terrace — the single most space-efficient extension type in London.

What a side extension is+

On a typical London Victorian terrace, there's a narrow strip — usually 900mm to 1.5 metres wide — running down one side of the house between the rear addition and the boundary wall. It's the remnant of the original scullery layout: too narrow to use, too expensive to ignore, and responsible for the dark, cramped feel of most Victorian ground floors.

A side return extension fills this strip with a new structure — usually a glazed roof or a flat roof with rooflights — and removes the wall between the old kitchen and the new space. The result is a significantly wider room that wraps around the corner of the house, bringing light deep into the plan where it never reached before.

On a typical mid-terrace, this adds between 8 and 15 square metres of usable floor area. It doesn't sound like a lot, but the effect is disproportionate: the kitchen goes from a narrow galley to a room wide enough for an island. The dining table moves from a separate room to the same space. The view to the garden, previously through a single window, wraps the corner.

Do you need planning permission?+

Most side return extensions on London terraces fall within permitted development rights because they're single-storey, under 4m in height, and within the 3m depth limit for attached houses. The width of the infill doesn't count against permitted development limits because it's measured from the rear wall, not the side wall.

However, if your property is in a conservation area, subject to an Article 4 direction, or if permitted development rights have been removed by a previous planning condition, you'll need a householder planning application. We check before any design work begins.

What it costs+

Our design fees for a side return extension typically range from £3,500 for a straightforward permitted development scheme to £14,000 for a larger side-and-rear combination that needs planning permission and full technical coordination. Fixed fee, agreed in writing, broken by RIBA stage.

Build costs for a side return infill in London are typically £2,500-£4,000 per square metre — a typical 12sqm side infill costs £30,000-£50,000 to build. The cost per metre is higher than a rear extension because the structural challenges are greater (you're building against the party wall and often under the existing first floor).

Side return vs rear extension vs wrap-around+

A side return is the smallest and cheapest of the three extension types, but often the most impactful relative to its cost because it transforms the feel of the ground floor rather than adding a new room. A rear extension adds more total space. A wrap-around combines both into one L-shaped volume — more expensive, but the most complete transformation of the ground floor.

We often recommend starting with a feasibility study that tests all three options side by side, with cost bands for each, so you can see what the money actually buys before committing to a direction.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a side return extension cost in London? +
Our design fees range from £3,500 to £14,000 depending on complexity. Build costs are typically £2,500-£4,000 per square metre — a typical 12sqm side infill costs £30,000-£50,000. The cost per metre is higher than a rear extension because the structural work is more complex.
Do I need planning permission for a side extension? +
Most side return extensions on London terraces fall within permitted development rights — single storey, under 4m height, within depth limits. If your property is in a conservation area or has an Article 4 direction, you'll likely need planning permission. We check your specific position before any design work.
How long does a side return extension take? +
Typically 3-4 months for design and consents, then 2-4 months on site. Side returns are quicker to build than rear extensions because the footprint is smaller, but the structural work (building against the party wall, supporting the floor above) requires careful sequencing.
Can I combine a side return with a rear extension? +
Yes — this is called a wrap-around extension. It creates an L-shaped ground floor that's wider (from the side return) and deeper (from the rear extension) in a single build. More expensive but the most complete transformation of a Victorian ground floor. We test both options in the feasibility study so you can compare.
