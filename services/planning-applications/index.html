Planning applications in London

Full planning submissions to every London borough and beyond — householder, full, outline, prior approval, and everything in between.

A planning application is the formal request to a local planning authority for permission to build. Most alterations to a property need one. Getting it wrong — wrong route, wrong documentation, wrong argument — costs months and, in the worst case, ends in a refusal that follows the address for years. We handle the whole process: feasibility, drawings, statements, submission, and the case-officer conversations that decide approval or refusal. Fees fixed in writing before we begin. First consultation free.

Permitted development or planning permission?+

Not every project needs a planning application. Permitted development rights grant a national form of consent for many common works — certain rear extensions, loft conversions, outbuildings and alterations — provided they stay within defined limits. The catch is that those rights are conditional, and they are reduced or removed in conservation areas, on listed buildings, for flats, and wherever an Article 4 direction applies.

Getting this judgement right at the outset is the most valuable thing we do. Where a project qualifies as permitted development we can confirm it with a Lawful Development Certificate; where it needs consent, we identify the correct application before any time is spent designing to the wrong route — so you proceed on solid ground rather than an assumption.

When you need one+

You need an application whenever the works are not covered by permitted development, or when those rights have been withdrawn by an Article 4 direction, a planning condition, or the property being in a conservation area, National Park or AONB.

In practice: most rear extensions above a certain depth, most side extensions, most changes of use, most work to flats, and almost all commercial work. Loft conversions often go through permitted development but the exceptions catch people out. We run the check as part of the free consultation.

The application types+

Householder — extensions and alterations to a single house. Shortest route.

Full — flats, new builds, commercial, changes of use, or works beyond householder scope.

Prior approval — where permitted development applies but the authority still checks size, siting and impact. Faster than full, but the tests are real.

Lawful development certificate — confirms works are lawful without needing planning permission. Useful for anything you may need to prove to a buyer or lender.

Pre-application — a paid conversation with the case officer before you submit. On borderline sites, almost always worth it.

What decides approval+

Applications are decided on the local plan and on material considerations — the NPPF, neighbourhood plans, heritage, daylight, ecology, highways. A good application evidences its compliance in the officer’s own language. A weak one leaves the officer to do that translation, which they rarely have time for.

The single biggest predictor of approval is not the design; it is the quality of the supporting statements. Most of our time goes on the argument, not the drawings.

Standards and regulations that apply+

The frameworks we work to as standard.

Town and Country Planning Act 1990

The primary legislation. Every application is made under it.

National Planning Policy Framework

Updated December 2024. Material to every decision.

Local Plan

The council’s own policy document, which carries statutory weight.

Neighbourhood Plans

Where adopted, carry the same weight as the local plan.

Article 4 Directions

Removes permitted development rights in specified areas. Common in London.

Building Safety Act 2022

Changed designer duties on all projects. Coordinated with planning where relevant.

Planning timescales and what to expect+

Once an application is validated, the Local Planning Authority has a statutory target of eight weeks to decide most householder and minor applications, and thirteen weeks for major development. In practice the clock only starts when the submission is complete and valid, which is why we take care to get the drawings, forms and supporting information right first time. Most straightforward proposals are decided by planning officers under delegated powers; larger or more sensitive schemes may go to a planning committee, which can add time. We keep the application moving, respond to the case officer’s queries promptly, and tell you honestly where a proposal stands.

If your application is refused+

A refusal is not the end of the road. Every decision comes with reasons, and those reasons determine the right response. Often a modest amendment resolves the officer’s concern and a fresh application succeeds; sometimes a different strategy is needed; and where a refusal is unreasonable, there is a right of appeal to the Planning Inspectorate. We review the decision, explain the realistic options and their likelihood, and only recommend a route we believe is worth pursuing — rather than resubmitting the same scheme and hoping for a different answer. Common questions

How long does an application take? +

Statutory eight weeks for a householder, thirteen for a full application, from validation. Total time from starting design to a decision is usually four to six months on a straightforward project.

What does it cost? +

The council’s application fee is a statutory amount (currently £258 for a householder application; more for full and commercial). Our fee is separate, fixed in writing before we begin.

What if it’s refused? +

Every refusal comes with reasons. Three routes usually follow: an amended resubmission (often at no extra fee within twelve months), a planning appeal to the Planning Inspectorate, or an alternative permitted-development route.

Do neighbours have to be consulted? +

Yes — the authority runs formal consultation. Objections don’t automatically defeat an application, but well-founded ones are weighted. On sensitive projects we recommend a five-minute conversation with neighbours before submission.

Can I appeal a refusal? +

Yes. Appeals go to the Planning Inspectorate and take four to nine months. Well-argued appeals win regularly.

Do I need permission to change use? +

Usually yes. Use Class E (introduced 2020) merged several former uses, making some changes automatic and others restricted.

What is permitted development? +

A set of national rights allowing certain works without an application. Detailed conditions apply, and many properties have had these rights removed by Article 4 or planning conditions.

Do you work outside London? +

Yes. English planning law is national. We work across the UK.
