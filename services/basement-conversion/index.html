Basement conversions in London

The most complex residential project type in London — and the one where getting the coordination right from day one matters most.

What a basement conversion involves+

A basement conversion creates a new habitable floor below your house — either by converting an existing cellar into a usable room, or by excavating from scratch beneath the existing ground floor. In London, where land values are high and lateral extension is constrained, going down is often the only way to add significant space.

It's also the most complex, most expensive, and most risk-laden residential project type. A rear extension involves building one new wall. A basement involves temporarily supporting the entire weight of your house while excavating the ground beneath it. The engineering is serious, the planning is stringent (especially in London, where several boroughs have introduced specific basement policies), and the party wall implications are substantial.

The two types+

Cellar conversion. If your house already has a cellar with adequate headroom (2.1m minimum, ideally 2.4m), converting it to a habitable room involves waterproofing (tanking), installing proper ventilation, upgrading the staircase to meet Building Regulations, and fitting it out. This is the cheaper and simpler route — typically £1,500-£2,500 per square metre for the build.

Full excavation (dig-down). If there's no existing cellar, or the headroom is insufficient, the entire floor below the house needs to be excavated — sometimes including underpinning the existing foundations. This is a major engineering project. Build costs are typically £3,000-£5,000 per square metre, and the work takes 6-12 months on site.

Planning in London+

Most London boroughs now require planning permission for basement excavations, and several have specific policies limiting the extent of basements (Camden, Kensington & Chelsea, Westminster in particular). These policies typically restrict basements to a single storey below the house, require natural light wells, limit the proportion of garden that can be excavated, and impose construction management conditions.

Getting planning consent for a London basement requires a strong Design and Access Statement, a Construction Management Plan, a Structural Method Statement, and often a Flood Risk Assessment. We coordinate all of these as part of the planning submission.

What it costs+

Our design fees for a basement conversion range from £8,000 for a simple cellar conversion to £25,000 for a full excavation with planning, party wall coordination, and detailed structural design. Build costs range from £100,000 for a basic cellar conversion to £300,000-£500,000+ for a full dig-down under a London townhouse.

Basement projects have the highest professional-fee-to-build-cost ratio of any residential project type because the coordination is so complex. Cutting corners on design is the single most reliable way to overspend on a basement build.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a basement conversion cost in London? +
Our design fees range from £8,000 to £25,000. Build costs range from £100,000 for a simple cellar conversion to £300,000-£500,000+ for a full dig-down. The cost depends on existing conditions, depth of excavation, waterproofing requirements, and borough-specific planning conditions.
Do I need planning permission for a basement in London? +
Almost always yes in London. Most boroughs now require planning permission for basement excavations. Some (Camden, RBKC, Westminster) have specific basement policies limiting size, depth and garden coverage. We navigate these borough-specific requirements as part of the service.
How long does a basement conversion take? +
6-12 months for design, planning and coordination, then 6-12 months on site. Basements are the longest residential project type — the excavation and underpinning alone can take 3-4 months. We give you a detailed programme at appointment.
Is a basement conversion worth it financially? +
In prime London postcodes (typically properties worth £1,500/sqft+), basement conversions consistently add more value than they cost. In outer London, the economics are harder to justify. We can give you a rough value-added estimate during feasibility based on comparable sales in your area.
