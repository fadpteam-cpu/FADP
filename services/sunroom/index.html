Sunroom design

Glazed extensions and sunrooms — planning strategy, thermal performance, structural design.

Bring more light and living space into your home. We design sunrooms and glazed additions that improve daylight, comfort and connection with the garden while responding carefully to the existing building.

What we provide+
  • Layout and concept designWhere the room sits, how it is shaped and how it connects to the rooms it opens from.
  • Glazing strategyHow much glass, where, and specified for daylight and comfort rather than glare or heat loss.
  • Connection to the existing houseThe threshold between old and new — often what decides whether the room feels right.
  • Planning adviceWhether the addition is permitted development or needs an application, established up front.
  • DrawingsThe plans, elevations and sections needed for planning and, later, construction.
  • Material and roof studiesRoof form and materials that sit comfortably with the existing building.
  • Technical coordinationStructure, insulation and detailing resolved so the room performs year-round.
What a sunroom adds to a home+

A well-designed sunroom does more than add floor area. Positioned and glazed with care, it draws daylight deep into the plan, creates a genuine connection to the garden, and gives the house a flexible room for relaxing, dining, working or entertaining across the year. The best examples read as a considered part of the house, not a bolted-on glass box.

Sunroom or conservatory?+

The distinction matters for comfort. A conservatory traditionally has a glazed roof, which can leave it too hot in summer and too cold in winter. A sunroom has a solid, insulated roof with full-height glazing to one or more walls — keeping the light and the garden views while holding a comfortable temperature year-round. Modern designs blur the two, and we specify glazing, roof and insulation to suit how you actually want to use the room.

Do you need planning permission for a sunroom?+

Often a sunroom can be built under permitted development, provided it stays within the limits on depth, height and position — but those rights are restricted on flats, in conservation areas and on listed buildings, and are removed where an Article 4 direction applies. We establish the route before design begins, and prepare a planning application or Lawful Development Certificate where one is needed.

What we consider+

Orientation and the path of the sun; the proportion of solid to glass; the threshold between the existing house and the new room; thermal performance and ventilation; the roof form; and how the addition reads from the garden. These are the decisions that separate a room used every day from one that sits empty half the year.

Types of sunroom+

Sunrooms come in several forms, and the right one depends on the house and how you want to use the space. A lean-to sunroom sits simply against the rear wall; a glazed link connects the house to the garden or to an outbuilding; an orangery-style room uses more solid structure and a lantern roof for a warmer, more room-like feel; and a contemporary garden-room hybrid blurs the line between sunroom and extension. We help you choose the form that suits the architecture of your home rather than a standard product.

Getting the most from the light+

Orientation changes everything. A south-facing sunroom gathers sun through the day but needs careful glazing and shading to stay comfortable; an east-facing room is bright in the morning; a west-facing room captures evening light; and even a north-facing room can be a calm, glare-free space to work. We study the path of the sun across your garden and position the glazing, roof and openings so the room is genuinely usable in every season, not just on mild days.

How much does a sunroom cost?+

As a guide, design fees for a sunroom typically fall between £2,500 and £6,000 depending on complexity, with build costs commonly in the region of £20,000 to £50,000 or more for a high-specification room. The largest variables are the extent of glazing, the roof construction, the ground conditions and the level of finish. We give you a clear fixed fee for the design work up front, and design to a budget you have set rather than leaving the cost to emerge at the end.

Building regulations for a sunroom+

Planning permission and building regulations are separate approvals, and a sunroom usually needs to satisfy both. Some glazed extensions are exempt from parts of the building regulations where they are thermally separated from the house and meet the criteria for a conservatory, but many modern sunrooms — used as everyday rooms — do not, and must meet standards for structure, energy efficiency, glazing safety and ventilation. We resolve this early so the room is comfortable, efficient and properly approved.

How long does a sunroom take?+

A typical sunroom runs from initial design to a completed room over several months. Concept and design usually take a few weeks; a planning application, where one is needed, adds around eight weeks for determination; and construction depends on size and specification. We set out a realistic programme at the start so you know what to expect at each stage.

Why start with a design practice+

Sunrooms are often sold as off-the-shelf products by installers, with the design decided by a standard catalogue rather than by your house. Starting with a design practice means the room is shaped around your building, your garden and the way you live — its proportions, its glazing, its connection to the existing rooms and its performance are all considered before anyone quotes for construction. You keep control of the design and the budget, and you end up with a room that feels part of the house rather than attached to it.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a sunroom cost? +
Design fees: £2,500-£8,000. Build costs: £25,000-£60,000 depending on size and glazing specification.
What is the difference between a sunroom and a conservatory? +
A sunroom has a solid insulated roof (better thermal performance). A conservatory traditionally has a glazed roof. Modern designs blur the boundary — we design both for year-round comfort.
