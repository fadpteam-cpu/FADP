Fees & getting started

What is the typical cost of design in the United Kingdom? +
There is no single standard fee. Fees depend on project size, complexity, scope, and stages of service. They may be fixed, percentage-based, time-based, or by stage. ARB confirms there is no standard tariff. Read our full guide to fees.
What is included in a fee? +
This depends on the agreed scope. Services can range from preparation and feasibility through concept, planning, spatial coordination, and technical design. Always check which stages, drawings, and deliverables are included in your fee proposal.
Do fees include planning permission? +
Not necessarily. The practice may prepare and submit a planning application, but the statutory application fee is a separate cost.
Do fees include Building Regulations? +
It depends on the appointment. Where technical design forms part of the service, we may prepare Building Regulations information and coordinate relevant technical requirements. Confirm in the fee proposal.
How does a project begin? +
With an initial consultation, in which a director reviews the site, the brief and the planning position. Where appropriate, a fixed-fee proposal follows.
How quickly are enquiries answered? +
Within one working day. A director replies to every enquiry personally.

The design process

What are the RIBA stages? +
The RIBA Plan of Work is the industry framework for design and construction. There are eight stages, from Stage 0 (strategic definition) to Stage 7 (in use). FADP typically focuses on Stages 1–4: preparation and briefing, concept design, spatial coordination, and technical design. See how we work.
How long does the whole process take? +
From first meeting to construction completion typically takes 12–18 months depending on complexity and planning time. Simple renovations may take 6–9 months. We provide a realistic programme at the start.
Is a survey required for the project? +
Depending on the property and scope, you may need a measured building, topographical, structural, drainage, or other specialist survey. The right survey should be carried out by a suitably qualified professional.
Should a designer be appointed before a builder? +
For many projects, yes. Architectural advice early helps establish scope, design, planning requirements, and technical needs before construction pricing is sought. This avoids expensive surprises later.
Does planning permission allow building work to begin? +
No. Planning permission and Building Regulations are separate matters. A project may require planning approval as well as compliance with Building Regulations and any other applicable requirements before construction proceeds.

Planning & permissions

Is planning permission required? +
It depends on the type of work and your location. Most extensions, renovations, and changes of use require planning permission. Some works fall within permitted development rights and do not. We advise you on what is required for your specific project.
How far can a property be extended? +
Local planning guidance typically allows extensions up to 20–30% of the original property floor area, depending on the council and circumstances. Conservation areas and listed buildings have stricter limits. We assess the property and advise on realistic possibilities.
What happens after a planning application is submitted? +
The local planning authority reviews the application against planning policy. They may ask for additional information. We handle all communication with the authority and keep you informed throughout. Applications typically take 8–13 weeks.
How can conservation area status be confirmed? +
Your local planning authority publishes conservation area maps online. If the property is in one, extra planning controls apply and permitted development rights may be restricted. Learn about conservation area work.
What is an Article 4 direction? +
An Article 4 direction removes some or all permitted development rights in a specific area, meaning you need planning permission for works that would otherwise be automatic. Common in conservation areas.

Party walls & boundaries

What is a party wall? +
A party wall is any wall shared between two properties. If you carry out work that affects a party wall, the Party Wall etc. Act 1996 typically applies. We coordinate with specialist party wall surveyors to ensure compliance.
Is a party wall agreement always required? +
Only if your work affects a party wall, party structure, or is close to a neighbouring building. Notifiable works include cutting into a shared wall, building near the boundary, or excavating within 3–6 metres of an adjacent building. Learn more.
What if a neighbour does not respond? +
If a neighbour does not respond within 14 days, they are deemed to be in dispute. A party wall surveyor is then appointed to prepare an award. The process continues, over a longer period.
Can the exact position of a boundary be established? +
Legal boundary positions rarely appear on Land Registry title plans, which are only indicative. Determining a precise boundary usually requires a Chartered Land Surveyor and consideration of historic records. Boundary dispute solutions.

Surveys & technical

How is a load-bearing wall identified? +
You cannot always tell from the surface. A structural engineer or designer surveys the property and checks floors above, direction of joists, and construction details. A professional assessment should always be obtained. Wall removal service.
Is Building Regulations approval required to remove a wall? +
Yes, for any load-bearing wall. Building Regulations apply to structural changes, fire safety, and often insulation and ventilation. We prepare drawings and calculations for approval.
A buyer's survey has identified movement. What follows? +
A structural report from a qualified professional determines whether movement is historic and stable, or active and requiring remedial work. We provide reports acceptable to lenders and insurers. Structural reports.
When should a snagging survey be done? +
On new-build homes, ideally before you complete legally, or within the first two years of ownership when the developer is still responsible for defects. Snagging surveys.
