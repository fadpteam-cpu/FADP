Mansard and upward extensions in London

Planning · 12 min read · Updated September 2026 · Aun Naeem

London has unused roofs and a shortage of homes. That is a planning argument, not a design. A mansard that is really a cheap extra floor parked on the ridge will still be refused. A mansard that continues a terrace already wearing the same hat is a different case.

Two different ideas

A mansard changes the roof: steep lower slope, shallow upper slope, typically a storey of rooms behind. Historically it is how London raised houses without pretending they were still a simple pitch.

An upward extension under national policy (including the Class AA additional-storeys route, where it still applies) is a different legal animal. It is not available on article 2(3) land — so not in conservation areas — and it comes with prior-approval tests on external appearance, overlooking and the effect on the street. Many inner-London houses are excluded before the drawing starts.

When a mansard is the right form

  • The terrace already has matching mansards, so the new work completes a pattern rather than inventing one.
  • The building historically had a roof storey that was later cut down.
  • A rear dormer would be a lump, and a mansard can hold a continuous eaves line along the street or the garden.

When it is the wrong form: an intact Georgian parapet, a roof that is the principal feature of a conservation area, or a house where the extra floor cannot take a legal stair and means of escape.

The geometry that usually survives committee

Hold the front eaves. Set the new storey back if the terrace demands it. Use slate, lead and dormer proportions that already exist on the roof, not a row of uPVC cabins. Party walls, fire, and structure are part of the first fee, not extras after the decision.

Hackney in particular has spent the last two years refusing mansards that ignore the SPD and then granting amended ones that sit down. The difference is rarely “more glass”. It is usually less bulk and better cheeks.

Permitted development is rarely the route

Class B roof enlargements are not permitted development in conservation areas. Class AA additional storeys are not available on article 2(3) land either. So most of inner London — the streets where people actually want a mansard — is a planning application, full stop.

Where Class AA still applies, prior approval tests external appearance, overlooking and the effect on the street. It is not a rubber stamp. We treat it as a design test, not a loophole.

Structure, fire, stairs

A mansard that cannot take a legal stair is a planning consent you cannot build. Means of escape, floor loads and party-wall matters sit in the first conversation, not after the decision. We bring a structural engineer in before the application is lodged.

If the only way to get a second staircase or a compliant bathroom is to eat the floor below, we show that on the options, so you can decide whether the extra storey is still the right brief.

What we need from you

Photographs of the front and rear roof, both neighbours’ roofs, and any previous drawings. If a terrace already wears matching mansards, that photograph is the argument. If it does not, we will say so before you pay for a full pack.
