Home · Introducers

Introduce a client. Earn a commission.

Surveyors, contractors, trades, and anyone else in the industry. A written commission on our professional fee. Agreed before we take the meeting.

If you send us a homeowner who appoints FADP, we pay you a commission on our professional fee for that appointment. It is never a percentage of build cost. The rate is written in a one-page letter before we take the meeting.

The rate is agreed in a one-page introducer letter before we take the meeting. If that letter is not in place, the lead is not counted.

Who it is for

Surveyors, contractors, builders, trades, estate agents, and anyone else who sits next to a homeowner who needs drawings or planning. London first. Manchester and Birmingham the same way.

How it works

  1. 01

    Email us the name, the address, and that you are introducing them.

  2. 02

    We send the introducer letter. You sign it. Then we take the meeting.

  3. 03

    If they appoint us, you earn the commission we wrote in the letter.

  4. 04

    We pay you when the client pays us for that stage.

The rules, short

The client can know. We will say an introducer is involved. Duplicate leads go to whoever emailed first with a signed letter. No commission if they do not appoint us. No commission on work they already had with us. You cannot introduce your own job as the client and collect the fee.

Common questions

What is the commission calculated on?

Our professional fee for the appointment they sign — the number in our letter, not the build cost. If the job grows and we issue a further stage, the same rate applies to that further fee unless we agree otherwise in writing.

Can the rate change by project?

Yes. The figure is written in the introducer letter before the first meeting. We do not renegotiate it after the client has appointed us.

When do I get paid?

When the client pays us for that stage. If they walk away, there is nothing to split.

Do I need to be a company?

No. We need a name, an email, and a way to pay you. A limited company invoice is fine. So is a sole trader.
